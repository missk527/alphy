Notes

1. The particle model of electromagnetic radiation

  • Electromagnetic radiation exhibits both wave-like and particle-like behaviour.
  • In the particle model, radiation transfers energy in discrete packets called photons.
  • Max Planck introduced the idea that energy exchange is quantized: it occurs in discrete amounts.
  • A wave inside a circle is a useful symbol for a photon because it represents both wave and particle properties.

2. Evidence: the photoelectric effect

  • Electrons can be emitted from a metal when electromagnetic radiation of sufficiently high frequency strikes its surface.
  • The minimum required frequency is the metal's threshold frequency.
  • The photoelectric effect provides evidence for the particle nature of electromagnetic radiation: individual photons transfer energy to electrons.

3. Photon energy and frequency

E = hf

  • E: energy of one photon, measured in joules (J).
  • h: Planck's constant, 6.63 × 10−34 J s (three significant figures).
  • f: frequency, measured in hertz (Hz), equivalent to s−1.
  • Photon energy is directly proportional to frequency: doubling frequency doubles energy.
  • From h = E/f, the units of h are J/(s−1) = J s.

Worked example: For f = 5.00 × 1014 Hz,
E = (6.63 × 10−34)(5.00 × 1014) = 3.32 × 10−19 J.

4. Photon energy and wavelength

Using c = fλ gives f = c/λ. Substituting into E = hf gives:

E = hc/λ

  • c: speed of light in a vacuum, 3.00 × 108 m s−1.
  • λ: wavelength in metres (m).
  • Photon energy is inversely proportional to wavelength.
  • Shorter-wavelength photons have greater energy; longer-wavelength photons have lower energy.
  • The equation connects wavelength, a wave property, with the energy of an individual photon, a particle property.

Worked example: A red laser emits light of wavelength 645 nm.
Convert first: 645 nm = 645 × 10−9 m.
E = (6.63 × 10−34)(3.00 × 108)/(645 × 10−9)
E = 3.08 × 10−19 J (three significant figures).

5. Summary

Photons are quanta of electromagnetic energy. Use E = hf when frequency is given and E = hc/λ when wavelength is given. Always convert wavelength to metres before calculating. For a fixed frequency, each photon has energy hf; the total energy of N identical photons is Nhf.
