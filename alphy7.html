ALPHY7. Coherence and Interference

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

Coherence and Stationary Waves
  • A stationary wave is formed when two progressive waves travelling in opposite directions interfere.
  • The two progressive waves must have the same frequency.
  • They must also maintain a constant phase relationship over time.
  • Coherent waves have the same frequency and a constant phase difference.
Constant Phase Relationship
  • A constant phase relationship does not require the waves to be in phase everywhere.
  • At one position, the waves may always be in phase and produce constructive interference.
  • At another position, they may always be in antiphase and produce destructive interference.
  • The phase difference at each position remains unchanged as time passes.
Stable Interference Patterns
  • When two progressive waves overlap, they interfere.
  • Coherent waves produce a stable interference pattern with fixed positions of constructive and destructive interference.
  • Two dippers vibrating at the same frequency can produce coherent circular water waves.
  • The wavelength is the distance between successive wavefronts.
Meaning of “Stable”
  • A stable interference point is not permanently a peak or permanently a trough.
  • At a point of constructive interference, the medium oscillates between a peak and a trough with maximum amplitude.
  • The type and position of interference remain fixed even though displacement changes with time.
Nodes and Antinodes
  • A stationary wave is an example of a stable interference pattern.
  • Nodes are fixed points of destructive interference and always have zero displacement.
  • Antinodes are fixed points of constructive interference and have maximum oscillation amplitude.
  • An antinode can momentarily have zero displacement while continuing to be a point of constructive interference.
Wave Directions
  • Coherent waves travelling in opposite directions can form a stable interference pattern.
  • Coherent waves that cross at a point can also form a stable interference pattern.
  • The waves do not have to travel in opposite directions; coherence is the essential condition.
Coherent and Incoherent Light Sources
  • A filament lamp emits a range of frequencies with a non-constant phase relationship, so it is incoherent.
  • Two filament lamps do not produce an observable stable pattern of fixed bright and dark regions.
  • Monochromatic light is a continuous electromagnetic oscillation at a single frequency.
  • A laser emits monochromatic light and is an example of a coherent source that can produce stable interference patterns.
