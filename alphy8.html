ALPHY8. Young’s Double-Slit Experiment and Light

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

Young’s Double-Slit Experiment
  • Interference occurs when two waves overlap and superpose; a stable interference pattern is evidence of wave behaviour.
  • Thomas Young used two narrow slits to produce two coherent light sources, S1 and S2.
  • Light diffracts at both slits, overlaps, and creates alternating bright and dark fringes on a screen.
Producing Coherent Light
  • A filament lamp emits white light containing many colours and frequencies. A colour filter selects approximately one colour, giving monochromatic light.
  • The first single slit acts as an effective point source: its diffracted light has a consistent phase across the beam.
  • Light from the single slit reaches both narrow slits, making their waves the same frequency with a constant phase difference.
  • A laser supplies highly monochromatic, coherent light and can be used instead of the filtered lamp.
Bright and Dark Fringes
  • A bright fringe marks constructive interference: waves arrive in phase and reinforce each other.
  • A dark fringe marks destructive interference: waves arrive in antiphase and cancel.
  • The fixed pattern of fringes demonstrates the wave nature of light.
Newton and the Colour Spectrum
  • Isaac Newton proposed a particle theory: light consists of particles he called corpuscles.
  • His theory could explain reflection and refraction.
  • A prism separates white light into a spectrum; Newton linked the colours to light itself and divided the spectrum into seven regions.
Huygens and Young: Wave Evidence
  • Christiaan Huygens proposed that light is a wave, with oscillations perpendicular to its direction of travel.
  • His wave theory could also explain reflection and refraction.
  • Young’s observed interference supplied evidence for the wave theory because interference is a wave property.
Laser Safety
  • A convex lens can focus a laser beam into a small spot, concentrating its intensity.
  • The eye’s lens can focus a direct or reflected laser beam onto the retina and damage it.
  • Never look along a laser beam, even after reflection. Use appropriate laser safety glasses when required.
