Notes

1. Progressive waves and key definitions

A progressive wave transfers energy from one place to another without a net transfer of matter. In a medium, particles oscillate about equilibrium while the disturbance travels.

  • Displacement: directed distance from equilibrium.
  • Amplitude: maximum magnitude of displacement from equilibrium.
  • Period (T): time for one complete oscillation, measured in seconds.
  • Frequency (f): number of complete oscillations per second, measured in hertz (Hz or s−1).
  • Wavelength (λ): shortest distance between two points in phase, such as adjacent crests, measured in metres.
f = 1/T     T = 1/f

Equal displacement alone does not establish equal phase. For an ideal progressive wave of constant amplitude, all particles have the same maximum displacement but reach it at different times.

2. Reading wave graphs

  • On a displacement–time graph, adjacent peaks are separated by one period.
  • On a displacement–distance graph, adjacent peaks are separated by one wavelength.
  • On either graph, amplitude is the vertical distance from equilibrium to a crest or trough.

3. The wave equation

Speed equals distance divided by time. A wave travels one wavelength in one period, so:

v = λ/T = fλ
  • v: wave speed in m s−1; f: frequency in Hz; λ: wavelength in m.
  • The symbol c is also used for wave speed, especially for light.
  • At constant speed, increasing frequency decreases wavelength.

Example: for f = 5 Hz and λ = 2 m, v = 5 × 2 = 10 m s−1.

4. Transverse and longitudinal waves

  • Transverse: oscillations are perpendicular to energy transfer. Examples include visible light and radio waves, both electromagnetic waves.
  • Longitudinal: oscillations are parallel to energy transfer. Examples include sound and ultrasound in air, and seismic P waves.
  • A slinky pushed and pulled along its length demonstrates longitudinal compressions and rarefactions. Moving its end sideways demonstrates transverse oscillations.

5. Polarization

Only transverse waves can be polarized. Longitudinal waves have no choice of transverse oscillation direction.

  • Unpolarized light: oscillations occur in many directions perpendicular to propagation, with no single preferred direction.
  • Plane-polarized light: electric-field oscillations are confined to one fixed transverse direction.
  • A polarizing filter transmits the electric-field component along its transmission axis and suppresses the perpendicular component.
  • Polarized sunglasses suppress much of the horizontally polarized glare reflected from water and also reduce overall light intensity.
  • A receiving antenna must be aligned with the polarization of the radio wave for strong reception.

6. Phase difference

For waves of the same frequency, phase difference describes their relative position within a cycle. A whole cycle corresponds to 360° or 2π radians.

  • In phase: zero phase difference, or any whole number of cycles.
  • Quarter cycle: 90° = π/2 radians.
  • Half cycle (antiphase): 180° = π radians.
  • Whole cycle: 360° = 2π radians, equivalent to being in phase.
Δφ = 2π(Δt/T)   or   2π(Δx/λ)

These expressions give phase difference in radians. Δt is the time offset between equal-frequency oscillations; Δx is the separation along a sinusoidal progressive wave at one instant.

To convert degrees to radians, multiply by π/180. To convert radians to degrees, multiply by 180/π.

1. What is a progressive wave?

Show answer

A wave that transfers energy from one place to another without net transfer of matter.

2. Do particles travel with a progressive wave in a medium?

Show answer

No. They oscillate about their equilibrium positions as energy travels through the medium.

3. Define the period of a wave.

Show answer

The time taken for one complete oscillation or cycle.

4. Define the frequency of a wave.

Show answer

The number of complete oscillations or cycles per second.

5. State the SI unit of frequency.

Show answer

The hertz (Hz), equivalent to s^-1.

6. Write the relationship between frequency f and period T.

Show answer

f = 1/T, or T = 1/f.

7. A wave has a period of 0.020 s. Calculate its frequency.

Show answer

f = 1/0.020 = 50 Hz.

8. A wave has a frequency of 25 Hz. Calculate its period.

Show answer

T = 1/25 = 0.040 s.

9. What is meant by the displacement of a particle?

Show answer

Its directed distance from its equilibrium position.

10. Define the amplitude of a wave.

Show answer

The maximum magnitude of displacement from equilibrium.

11. A particle moves between +4 mm and -4 mm. What is its amplitude?

Show answer

4 mm.

12. Define wavelength precisely.

Show answer

The shortest distance between two points on a wave that are in phase, such as adjacent crests.

13. What does the separation between adjacent peaks on a displacement-time graph represent?

Show answer

The period T.

14. What does the separation between adjacent peaks on a displacement-distance graph represent?

Show answer

The wavelength.

15. Why can wavelength not be read directly from the horizontal axis of a displacement-time graph?

Show answer

The horizontal axis measures time, whereas wavelength is a distance.

16. What distance does a progressive wave travel in one period?

Show answer

One wavelength.

17. Use speed = distance/time to derive the wave equation.

Show answer

In one period T, a wave travels one wavelength lambda. Thus v = lambda/T = f lambda.

18. State the SI units of wave speed and wavelength.

Show answer

Wave speed: m/s. Wavelength: m.

19. A wave has frequency 5 Hz and wavelength 2 m. Calculate its speed.

Show answer

v = f lambda = 5 x 2 = 10 m/s.

20. Sound travels at 340 m/s with frequency 170 Hz. Calculate its wavelength.

Show answer

lambda = v/f = 340/170 = 2.0 m.

21. A wave travels at 12 m/s and has wavelength 0.30 m. Calculate its frequency.

Show answer

f = v/lambda = 12/0.30 = 40 Hz.

22. At constant wave speed, what happens to wavelength when frequency doubles?

Show answer

The wavelength halves, because lambda = v/f.

23. Define a transverse wave.

Show answer

A wave whose oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer.

24. Define a longitudinal wave.

Show answer

A wave whose oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer.

25. Give two examples of transverse waves.

Show answer

Visible light and radio waves; both are electromagnetic waves.

26. Give two examples of longitudinal waves.

Show answer

Sound in air and seismic P waves. Ultrasound in air is also acceptable.

27. How can a slinky demonstrate a longitudinal wave?

Show answer

Push and pull one end along its length, producing compressions and rarefactions that travel along it.

28. How can a slinky demonstrate a transverse wave?

Show answer

Move one end perpendicular to its length; the disturbance travels along the slinky.

29. What is meant by unpolarized light?

Show answer

Light with oscillations in many different directions perpendicular to its direction of propagation, with no single preferred direction.

30. What is meant by plane-polarized light?

Show answer

Light whose electric-field oscillations are confined to one fixed direction perpendicular to propagation.

31. Which type of wave can be polarized?

Show answer

Transverse waves.

32. Why can longitudinal waves not be polarized?

Show answer

Their oscillations are along the propagation direction, so there are no different transverse oscillation directions to select.

33. What does a polarizing filter do to incident light?

Show answer

It transmits the electric-field component along its transmission axis and suppresses the perpendicular component.

34. How do polarized sunglasses reduce glare from water?

Show answer

Reflected glare is often strongly horizontally polarized. A suitably oriented filter suppresses that component, reducing glare.

35. Why must a receiving antenna be correctly aligned with a polarized radio wave?

Show answer

Its orientation must match the wave polarization for strong reception; a perpendicular orientation greatly reduces the received signal.

36. What does phase difference describe for two waves of the same frequency?

Show answer

How far their oscillations are offset within a cycle, expressed as an angle in degrees or radians.

37. Express a quarter-cycle phase difference in degrees and radians.

Show answer

90 degrees, or pi/2 radians.

38. Express a half-cycle phase difference in degrees and radians.

Show answer

180 degrees, or pi radians; the oscillations are in antiphase.

39. What phase difference corresponds to one complete cycle, and are the oscillations in phase?

Show answer

360 degrees, or 2 pi radians. Yes, this is equivalent to zero phase difference.

40. A wave has period 0.80 s. A second wave of the same frequency is delayed by 0.20 s. Find the phase difference.

Show answer

The delay is 0.20/0.80 = 1/4 cycle, so the phase difference is 90 degrees or pi/2 radians. The second wave lags.
