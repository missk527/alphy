ALPHY4. Plane Polarisation

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

Transverse Waves and Direction
  • In a transverse wave, the direction of oscillation is perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
  • A wave may travel in either of two opposite directions, but its propagation must remain perpendicular to its oscillation.
  • When the wave travels into or out of the screen, its oscillation can occur at many angles within the plane of the screen.
Understanding Plane Polarisation
  • Polarisation describes the plane in which a transverse wave oscillates.
  • The plane of polarisation is perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
  • A wave is plane polarised, or linearly polarised, when its oscillation remains in one plane.
  • A wave oscillating in more than one plane is unpolarised.
Polarising a Wave Through a Slit
  • A wave on a rope passing through a slit provides a useful model of polarisation.
  • An unpolarised transverse wave contains oscillations in many directions.
  • After passing through a slit, only the oscillation parallel to the slit is transmitted.
  • The transmitted wave is therefore linearly polarised.
Parallel and Perpendicular Orientations
  • If the slit is parallel to the plane of polarisation, the wave passes through unaffected.
  • If the slit is perpendicular to the plane of polarisation, the wave is blocked.
  • These results show that transmission depends on the relative orientation of the wave and the slit.
Polarisation at an Intermediate Angle
  • If the wave has a component parallel to the slit, that component can pass through.
  • After transmission, the new plane of polarisation is parallel to the slit.
  • Only part of the original wave is transmitted, so its amplitude is reduced.
  • A lower amplitude produces a lower transmitted intensity.
Polarising Filters
  • All transverse waves follow the same basic polarisation rules.
  • In practice, polarising filters are commonly used to produce linearly polarised waves.
  • An unpolarised wave becomes linearly polarised after passing through a suitable filter.
Electromagnetic Waves and Filter Molecules
  • Polarising filters contain long chains of organic molecules.
  • These molecules absorb electromagnetic-field components parallel to the molecular chains.
  • The transmitted plane of polarisation is therefore perpendicular to the lines representing the molecules on the filter.
  • The filter lines indicate the absorption direction, not the transmitted polarisation direction.
